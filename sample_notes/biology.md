# Cellular respiration overview
Cellular respiration turns the chemical energy in glucose into ATP, the energy currency of the cell.
It has three main stages: glycolysis, the Krebs cycle (citric acid cycle) and the electron transport chain.
Overall, one glucose molecule and oxygen produce carbon dioxide, water and roughly 30 to 32 ATP.

# Glycolysis
Glycolysis happens in the cytoplasm and does not need oxygen.
It splits one glucose (6 carbons) into two pyruvate molecules (3 carbons each).
The net gain is 2 ATP and 2 NADH per glucose.

# Krebs cycle
In cells with oxygen, pyruvate enters the mitochondrial matrix and is converted to acetyl-CoA, releasing CO2.
Each turn of the Krebs cycle produces NADH and FADH2, which carry electrons to the next stage, plus one ATP (or GTP).
The cycle turns twice per glucose because glycolysis made two pyruvates.

# Electron transport chain
The electron transport chain sits in the inner mitochondrial membrane.
NADH and FADH2 hand their electrons to a chain of proteins that pump protons into the intermembrane space.
Protons flow back through ATP synthase, which uses that flow to make most of the cell's ATP.
Oxygen is the final electron acceptor and combines with protons to form water.

# Fermentation
Without oxygen, cells regenerate NAD+ by fermentation so glycolysis can keep running.
Muscle cells make lactate; yeast makes ethanol and CO2. Fermentation yields only the 2 ATP from glycolysis.

# Photosynthesis
Photosynthesis in the chloroplast stores light energy in sugar. The light reactions in the thylakoid membranes
split water, release oxygen and make ATP and NADPH. The Calvin cycle in the stroma uses that ATP and NADPH
to fix CO2 into sugar, with the enzyme rubisco doing the carbon fixation step.
